What it is.

Throttlr lives between your app and the network. Pick any running Windows process — a game, a chat client, a download manager, your browser, whatever — and Throttlr intercepts every packet that process tries to send or receive.

Once intercepted, you decide what happens: drop them, delay them, cap their bandwidth, hold them in a queue and release them in a burst, or block them entirely. All in real time. All without touching any other app on your machine.

Built on the WinDivert kernel driver. Open-source. Free. Windows 10 and 11. No accounts, no telemetry, no servers — everything runs on your own machine.

Six functions.

Mix freely. Toggle independently. Apply inbound, outbound, or both.

01

Lag

Add controllable latency to any app's traffic. Set a target millisecond delay, optionally with jitter for realism. Find out what 200ms ping actually feels like in your favorite game.

200 ms
02

Drop

Discard packets at a configurable rate, from a 1% drizzle to a total wipeout. Optional DNS-only mode for surgical disruption — drop the domain lookups, let everything else through.

25%
03

Throttle

Hard bandwidth ceiling in KB/s, applied via a token bucket algorithm. Smooth ratelimit, no spiky surprises. 50 KB/s for a 56k modem. 200 KB/s for slow 3G. 5 MB/s for cable.

100 KB/s
04

Hold every packet in a kernel-level queue while frozen. When you toggle off, all 5,000 held packets release in a single rapid burst. Up to one million packets in queue.

5,234 held
05

Total stop. Inbound, outbound, or both. The kill switch when you need silence. Useful for testing how an app handles sudden disconnects.

100% blocked
06

Fun

Glitch mode. Random reorder, duplicate, corrupt, fragment. Watch your app cope. Or not. Crank the intensity slider and see what breaks first.

// 03

Per-process. Not system-wide.

Most network throttling tools work at the system level — their filters apply to all traffic that matches a tcpdump-style expression. Useful, but blunt. They can't say "throttle only this one app while leaving everything else alone" without you knowing the exact IPs and ports involved.

Throttlr matches by process ID. A second kernel-level handle listens for connection events the moment they're created, building a real-time map of which flows belong to which app. There's no polling, no timing window where new connections slip through unfiltered. Every packet a targeted process touches gets caught.

And because it's all running at the kernel level, the targeted app sees real network conditions. There's nothing to detect, nothing to bypass. From the app's perspective, your connection just got worse.

Advanced tools.

For when the toggles aren't enough.

Connection Inspector

Live table of every connection from the targeted app. Hostname (parsed straight from TLS SNI), country, protocol, ports, bytes up & down, age, idle time.

Domain & Geo Block

Built-in blocklists for ads, trackers, and telemetry. Block whole regions with the country picker. Add your own custom domains. Scope: just the targeted app.

Practice Ping

Find out what high-ping gameplay feels like. Six presets from LAN 5ms to Unplayable 400ms, or set a custom target. Calibrated jitter for realism.

Recording & Replay

Capture an entire session — every stat tick, every config change — into a single file. Scrub the timeline, hit play, change speed (0.25× to 10×).

Network Topology

Live force-graph of your app's connections. Your machine sits at the center, every remote endpoint orbits around it. Animated traffic dots ride each edge.

PCAP Capture

Dump raw packets to a standard .pcap file. Open straight in Wireshark, tcpdump, or tshark. Captures only the targeted app — no cross-process noise.

Filter Script

Write your own per-packet filter expressions in a Python-like DSL. Sandboxed via AST whitelist. Variables for ports, hostnames, packet size, country.

Quick Presets

One-tap configurations. Vibes (Connection Killer, DNS Block) and real-world profiles (56k Modem, 3G, Cable, Fiber, Satellite). Save your own.
